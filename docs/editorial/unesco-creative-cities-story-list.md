# Destination story list — UNESCO Creative Cities alignment

Researched 2026-10-09 for the CSV destination list ("Folkly - write up list").
Every designation below was verified against the UNESCO Creative Cities Network
member roster (unesco.org/en/creative-cities/list) and UNESCO's own city pages /
monitoring reports; cohort lists (2025, 2023, 2021, 2019, 2017, 2015, earlier)
were cross-checked in full.

## How to read this

- **UNESCO field** = the city's verified UCCN designation (year). Where the CSV's
  assumption was wrong, it is corrected here.
- **Not a UCCN member** = the city does not appear anywhere in the UNESCO roster.
  For those, the pitch uses the strength the list attributes to the city (or a
  distinctive, well-documented living practice) — flagged so we don't claim a
  designation we can't cite.
- Pitches are **angles to research**, not asserted facts: each names the practice,
  the place, and where the named local voices live. The pipeline's evidence gates
  verify specifics before publication.
- Persona fit: **Mira Sol** (making/craft economies), **Ellis Reed** (sound),
  **Lena March** (food/community life), **Sasha Wren** (ritual/ceremony),
  **Rowan Pike** (urban ecology/infrastructure).

## Notes for the editorial calendar

- **Strongest UNESCO-fit openers** (designation + distinctive practice + strong
  documentation trail): Barcelona (Sant Jordi), Reykjavík (Jólabókaflóð), Dundee
  (comics), Kingston (sound systems), Guadalajara (media arts), Medellín (design),
  Bristol (film), Chiang Mai (silver), Edinburgh (literature), Santa Fe (Indian Market).
- **Corrections to the CSV worth knowing**: São Paulo is Film (2025), Milan is
  Literature (2017), Brno is Music (2017), Guadalajara is Media Arts (2017);
  Nashville, Vienna, Copenhagen, Florence, Kyoto, Marrakech, Oaxaca, Yogyakarta,
  Lagos, Porto, Amsterdam, Tokyo, London, Paris, NYC, LA, SF, Portland,
  Wollongong, Córdoba, Lisbon are not in the network at all.
- **Geographic balance**: the member-city openers skew strongly outside North
  America/Europe (Medellín, Guadalajara, Chiang Mai, Kingston, Mumbai, Valparaíso,
  Dubai, Seoul, Mexico City, Auckland, Melbourne), which supports the ≥50%
  rolling-month rule.
- **Audience fit** (from the CSV's user bases: digital nomads, cultural tourists,
  creative cities, tour companies): the working-place framing (studios, workshops,
  ateliers, sound systems) gives tour operators and creative-sector readers an
  itinerary hook — every pitch names a visitable scene, not just an idea.
- **Overlap with the existing archive**: Detroit (Design, 2015), New Orleans
  (Music, 2025), Lisbon, and Oaxaca are already published or in reserve; their
  follow-up angles above are deliberately different practices per the
  "a return to a place needs a substantially different practice" rule.
