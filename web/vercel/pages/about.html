About Folkly

Stories about the intersection
of Culture and Place.

A procession, a weaving practice, a music scene: each opens a story about people, history, and belonging. Folkly explores what makes a place distinctive and what allows its cultural life to grow, change, and endure.

The journal grows out of Noah Rappaport’s Creative Cities project. His work in creative direction and systems thinking informs a curiosity about the relationships beneath everyday expression: who makes it, what sustains it, and what it makes possible for others.

Identity is something we keep making.

Noah’s framework, Adaptive Identity Through Struggle, asks how communities respond to change and make an inherited identity their own. A new technology, an economic pressure, or an encounter with another culture can unsettle familiar ways of living. People negotiate what to preserve, what to borrow, and what to create.

The adaptive identity cycle

Five connected moments offer a way into these questions. They are prompts for inquiry, not a sequence every community must follow.

01 Encounter & disruption

A changing environment brings familiar ways of living into contact with new conditions.

02 Identity negotiation

People reconsider what belongs, adapting outside influences or protecting a meaningful distinction.

03 Creative response

Music, food, craft, ritual, design, and practical invention give a response its form.

04 Integration

Repeated, taught, and supported, a new practice becomes part of ordinary life.

05 Renewed identity

A changed understanding becomes an inheritance that another generation can take forward.

Struggle is central to this working theory, but hardship does not guarantee growth. Its severity, the support available, and the possibility of loss all matter. Creative activity can meet practical needs, deepen belonging, and open new questions at once; these are overlapping purposes, not a hierarchy of cultures.

How we approach a story

Begin with a particular place and practice. Ask which people, relationships, resources, and histories make it possible. Our essays link their sources and distinguish documented history from interpretation. Local terms receive context, and differences within communities remain part of the story.

People and evidence can challenge the framework. The question stays open: what does a community carry forward, and what does it make possible next?
